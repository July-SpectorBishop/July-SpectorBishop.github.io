PERadoxical Cert-ainty in SNAP: Rethinking the Accuracy-Access Tradeoff

July Spector-Bishop  ·  Working Paper, 2026

Recent federal policy changes have compelled states to prioritize payment accuracy over access to benefits in the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP). Some states have required participants to recertify more frequently to verify eligibility, but such requirements threaten to reduce program takeup through increasing administrative burdens. I find preliminary evidence that shorter certification periods can have the opposite of their intended effect: increasing the administrative workload faced by state agencies, thus leading to more errors. Using public-use SNAP Quality Control data, I show that eligible non-elderly households are more likely to have a payment error if they certified during a quarter in which state agencies had to process more recertifications. This paper contributes to the sparse literature on the causes of SNAP Payment Error Rates and re-frames payment accuracy as the output of overall administrative quality rather than one side of an accuracy-access tradeoff.
